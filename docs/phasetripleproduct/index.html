A map of the Lean proofs for PhaseTripleProduct

A recursive Toom–Cook construction for a three-register phase, and what is proved about it

Scope: read this first

This page is about a different primitive from the one in ForShor's order-finding circuit. PhaseProduct applies \(\exp(i\pi\varphi\,xz)\) to two registers and is what ForShor's modular multiplication is built from. PhaseTripleProduct (PTP) applies \(\exp(i\pi\varphi\,xyz)\) to three. ForShor's correctness and gate-count theorems do not use it, and nothing here changes them.

The development is part of ForShor, under Implementation/PhaseTripleProduct/, in the same layout as PhaseProduct/: Statement.lean defines everything and states the claims, Proofs/ holds the lemmas, and Main.lean proves the claims. Read those two files and you know what is claimed. It is on the branch ptp/phasetripleproduct and is not merged into main yet; the source links on this page point at a pinned commit of that branch. See where it lives.

A phase triple product multiplies a basis state by \(\exp(i\pi\varphi\,x\,y\,z)\), where \(x,y,z\) are the signed integers held in three \(n\)-qubit registers. Done directly, with a doubly-controlled phase for each triple of bits, it costs about \(n^3\) gates. The recursive construction is ForShor's PhaseProduct one with a third factor. Split each operand into \(k\) limbs; the product of three degree-\((k-1)\) limb polynomials has degree \(3(k-1)\), so its value at the limb radix is a fixed rational combination of its values at \(3(k-1)+1 = 3k-2\) distinct points. Evaluating the three operands at each point with a short program of additions, negations and shifts, taking one smaller phase triple product per point, and recursing gives \(O(n^{\log_k(3k-2)})\) gates for a fixed table: \(n^{2}\) at \(k=2\), \(n^{1.77}\) at \(k=3\), tending to \(n\).\(\log_k(3k-2)\) for \(k = 2,3,4,5,6\): \(2.000,\ 1.771,\ 1.661,\ 1.609,\ 1.547\). The PhaseProduct exponent \(\log_k(2k-1)\) is \(1.585,\ 1.465,\ 1.404,\ 1.365,\ 1.343\). The comparison is between the exponents of the two constructions, not between two ways of computing the same thing.

The table is the same object as in PhaseProduct: the Framework's Prog k, a straight-line program of addScaled, negate, shiftL, shiftR and phaseProduct i checkpoints, with its interpolation points. What changes is the point count (\(3k-2\) rather than \(2k-1\)) and what a checkpoint means: each is taken on all three operands. The development verifies this at the gate level in ForShor's LowGate language, for whole banded policies, and states its claims as propositions in one file; the central one: for every checked policy and every width there is a circuit following the policy that acts as the specification operator on every clean state, with an exact gate census. Extensions give a controlled version, the \(O(n^{\log_k(3k-2)})\) gate count, operands of unequal width and a qubit count.

Below, the construction first as a flow, then the proof as a graph of layers, then the recursion itself as a figure, then the theorems, what is not claimed, and how to check it. Every block links back to the Lean source at a recorded line.

How one node is computed

The whole construction is one step, repeated. Given three registers and a width, the policy either stops at a leaf or picks a table; the registers are split into limbs, moved to each interpolation point by the table's program, a smaller phase triple product is taken there, and everything is undone. Each of the \(3k-2\) smaller products is the same problem at a smaller width, which is the loop on the left. Click a step for what it does and the Lean statement that carries it.

The proof, layer by layer

The development follows ForShor's own layout. Statement holds every definition the claims mention and the claims themselves; Main proves them; the blocks between the two are the lemmas (Proofs/). A reader needs only those two files, and building Main.lean is the guarantee that the claims are proved. Every arrow below is a real import, read from the Lean sources by this site's generator, and no arrow points up the figure. The rule that makes Statement plus Main enough is checked rather than asserted: scripts/StatementClosure.lean computes every constant the claims reach, unfolding definitions, and fails if one lives outside Mathlib, Framework/ or a Statement.lean, and CI runs it. Open a block for its role, the files in it, what it rests on and feeds, and the statements it carries, quoted verbatim.

Every block between Statement and Main imports Statement directly, so only the arrows that carry something are drawn. The spine is Support → Table → Values → Gates → Recursion → ExactBoard → Surface. The mathematics (node_identity) comes before any gate. The recursion exists twice, over the conservative scan widths and over the exact widths, and the headline theorem is stated at the exact ones. Everything after Surface is an extension, a witness, or the proof of the claims.

Inside PTP: one table, applied recursively

The layer graph does not show how the circuit is built. The figure below draws the reference policy of the benchmark (policies/ref_ptp_2048.json in the ShorTableOptimization fork, where the benchmark lives; it is input to this figure, not part of the Lean development), at \(n = 2048\). Its four bands use \(k = 6, 4, 2, 2\); at exact widths the recursion goes \(2048 \to 375 \to 189 \to 96 \to 50\), using the first, third, third and fourth bands and then the leaf, so the \(k = 4\) band is never reached. The middle panel draws the \(k = 2\) table of the third band, which splits one product into four, and the right panel what happens to each.

One recursion level. \(\Pi^{(0)}\) turns \((x_0, x_1)\) into \((x_0, x_0+x_1)\); two phase triple products are taken, at \(p=1\) on \(r_1\) and at \(p=0\) on \(r_0\). \(\Pi^{(1)}\) moves the registers to \((x_0 - x_1,\ x_1)\), for \(p=-1\) and \(p=\infty\). \(\Pi^{(2)}\) adds \(r_1\) back into \(r_0\), restoring \((x_0, x_1)\). Each product has its own angle, \(\varphi\,c_t\), with \(c_t\) the interpolation weight of its point at the limb radix \(B\): for this table \(c = (B+B^2)/2,\ 1-B^2,\ (B^2-B)/2,\ B^3-B\), and node_identity says they combine the four products into \(\varphi\,xyz\). The arithmetic is shared by all three operands, so a table operation becomes three gates, one per operand.

Why this has to be checked. A table is trusted only through its contract: \(3k-2\) distinct valid points, each checkpoint consuming the next point in order with the registers holding exactly that point's values, and the program returning the registers to where they started. The fault witnesses show the conditions are load-bearing. Declaring the points in the wrong order is rejected by the contract; and evaluated anyway it applies \(B^3+3B^2-5B+1\) rather than \((B+1)^3\) at \(x=y=z=B+1\). Running the children one bit below the exact width gives a circuit that is provably wrong on a concrete state, because an intermediate value overflows and is then read by a child.

What is proved

Statements are quoted at the strength Lean proves them. The theorem names are those of Main.lean, each stated as exactly the proposition of the same name in Statement.lean. Every row depends on at most the axioms propext, Classical.choice and Quot.sound: scripts/AxiomCheck.lean asserts it for all nine claims (CI runs it), and a trusted kernel replay re-checked every module of the development on exactly those axioms (see the review status below). The links go to the line in the source.

TheoremWhat it says

Main		 For every checked policy \(P\), width \(n\) and three disjoint \(n\)-qubit registers there is a circuit following \(P\) at exact widths that acts as the specification \(\exp(i\pi\varphi\,x_0x_1x_2)\) on every clean state (operands fit signed \(n\) bits, workspace \(|0\rangle\)), with the exact-board gate census as equalities and a qubit upper bound. For \(n=0\) the state-level conjunct is vacuous.

Values		 The weighted sum over the \(3k-2\) declared points of the products of the three operands' point values equals \(\varphi\,x_0x_1x_2\), for every admissible table.

Values		 The exact width is minimal: one bit fewer and some operand that fits \(n\) bits has a reachable register value that does not fit.

ExactBoard		 Existence of the recursion for every checked policy and width at exact widths, with correctness and the census.

Accept		 The acceptance rule without the ladder: every table chosen at every width the exact recursion from \(n\) reaches has \(k \le m\) and strictly shrinks the width.

Controlled		 Controlled PTP by masking one operand, on the clean subspace: \(2n\) Toffolis (\(n\) of them \(\dagger\)), \(n+1\) extra qubits, no extra rotations.

Unbalanced		 Operands of unequal width, by in-place sign-extension padding: \(+2(d_0+d_1+d_2)\) CNOTs, qubits \(\le 3n\) plus workspace.

Asymptotic		 For a constant policy the exact-board gate count (the census total) is \(O(n^{\log_k(3k-2)})\) under shorGateResourceModel.

Asymptotic		 For a constant policy there is a circuit following it with \(O(n^{\log_k(3k-2)})\) gates. Existential: see what is not claimed.

QubitsTight		 There is a circuit following the policy touching at most \(3n + \texttt{ptpWsTD}\) distinct qubits; ptp_qubits_tight_exact and ptp_qubits_le_ptpQubits are the exact-board and scan-board forms (ptpQubits is a proved upper bound at the scan rule).

Model		 The headline claim specialised to ForShor's concrete Model.qs: the hypotheses are satisfiable.

What is not claimed

  • Not part of order finding. ForShor's circuit uses PhaseProduct. These theorems do not enter its correctness or its \(n^{2+\varepsilon}\) bound.
  • Clean states only. The state-level theorems cover the span of clean kets. For \(n=0\) no ket is clean, so the state-level conjunct is vacuous (the circuit, the workspace bound and the census are still required).
  • Controlled PTP is the masked construction. ForShor-style leaf control, where every leaf phase is controlled, is a different construction and is not proved here. In the masked statement the control is not constrained against the other registers; the degenerate case is the identity on the clean span.
  • The gate count is for a constant policy (one table at every width), as in ForShor, and counts rz + toffoli + x + h + cnot under shorGateResourceModel (Cuccaro adders, arbitrary-angle rotations). It is not a T count. The multi-band reference policy is verified for correctness and census, not given an asymptotic bound.
  • The gate-count and qubit claims are existential. PTPCircuitGateCountBound, PTPQubitsTight, PTPQubitsTightExact and PTPQubitsLePtpQubits each say that some circuit following the policy has that count. None says it is the circuit of PTPLowerExactCorrect or that it is correct; correctness and the census are proved separately, for every circuit of the form PolicyImplX, but no single claim bundles the two.
  • Qubits. The theorems bound LowGate.usedQubits, not the framework's qubitCount, which adds the Cuccaro/negation scratch cleanAnc; that scratch is not bounded for the recursive circuit. ptpQubits is loose by up to a factor \(k\) per level.
  • Unequal widths are padded, not handled by a native unbalanced node. Open: that node, the least width at which the wrapping semantics is correct, and the leaf-control variant.
  • No optimality statement. No table is claimed optimal, and the benchmark scores PTP targets by the structural score only (below). The policy checker and planner of the ShorTableOptimization fork are separate from this development and are not covered by these claims.
  • Review status. Verified on ForShor qubitCount (6d55ad9): the full CI build (every target, warnings counted as failures) passes; the axiom guard asserts all nine claims on propext, Classical.choice, Quot.sound; a trusted kernel replay (the autoformalizer toolkit's, one module per process) re-checked all 28 modules of the development, 2,141 declarations, on exactly those axioms. An independent statement-fidelity review compared Statement.lean with the original suite this was ported from and found no difference in meaning; it noted that the gate-count and qubit claims are existential, which is stated above. The one CI step that fails is the docs-graph check, which already fails on an unmodified qubitCount (KeyError: 'Shared': its folder list and curated annotations predate that branch's reorganisation). No human review; the original suite was additionally audited by independent AI reviewer passes whose record is in the ShorTableOptimization fork.

The benchmark targets

ShorTableOptimization asks for tables at \(k = 2,\dots,16\) for each of two modes. A table's score is arithmetic operations + 5 × parallel phase-product layers, a convention that trades size against depth. The benchmark prices PhaseTripleProduct structurally only: upstream has no gate model for it, so these scores are never T counts or gate counts. Checking it yourself

In a checkout of ForShor with elan installed, on the branch above. The Mathlib build cache is fetched, so only ForShor itself compiles; a cold lake build ForShor took about 8.5 minutes and 4.7 GB peak on an M-series Mac before this development was added. 

lake exe cache get
lake build ForShor.ShorVerification.Implementation.PhaseTripleProduct.Main
lake env lean scripts/StatementClosure.lean   # every constant the claims reach is in Mathlib/core, Framework/ or a Statement.lean
lake build ForShor && lake env lean scripts/AxiomCheck.lean   # #assert_axioms on all nine claims and the width fault

The ShorTableOptimization fork has, separately, a harness that takes a policy as JSON and has the kernel decide the check and the exact-width acceptance rule (scripts/check_ptp_policy.py), and a Python mirror of the exact-width ladder (scripts/ptp_exact_width.py). They are not part of ForShor and not covered by the claims above; the reference policy and the ladder in the figure come from there. 

# in the ShorTableOptimization fork
python3 scripts/ptp_exact_width.py policies/ref_ptp_2048.json 2048     # the exact and scan ladders, no Lean needed

This page's graph and figure are generated from the Lean sources by scripts/gen_ptp_site.py (--suite points at a ForShor checkout; --check fails if the data on disk is stale; it refuses a dirty checkout, and any file that is not tracked at the pinned commit). The figure's table is executed by the generator, which also re-checks the node identity on it by exact arithmetic.
